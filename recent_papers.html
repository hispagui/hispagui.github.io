Some recent papers that caught my attention

The first paper that I found really cool was this one: The Mathieu group \(M_{23}\) is a Galois group over \(\mathbb{Q}\) by
Xiaoyu Huang, Blake Jackson, Kyu-Hwan Lee, Bjorn Poonen, Rachel Pries and Shaowu Zhang. For a bit of context, Galois theory attaches to every polynomial with rational coefficients a finite group, its Galois group, it records the symmetries among the polynomial's roots. The inverse Galois problem turns this arround and asks, given a finite group \(G\), does there exist a polynomial over \(\mathbb{Q}\) whose Galois group is the same as \(G\)? This problem is quite hard, as of today very open, and has already been extensively studied. It is known that alternating groups, abelian groups and solvable groups are all realisable as Galois groups from a polynomial (to be more precise, we say a finite Galois extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\)). Nonetheless, there are many, many, many more groups for which the problem remains open.

A natural place to test the problem is for a special set of groups, the finite simple groups. They are kind of the building blocks from which all finite groups are assembled. The classification of finite simple groups (also the longest mathematical proof in history!) says that each finite simple group belongs to a few infinite families:
cyclic groups of prime order, alternating groups, groups of Lie type and sporadic groups.

My interest today does not lie in explaining what all this groups are, nor talk about this classification of finite simple groups. But that last familiy is the one we are interested in. To put it simply, sporadic groups are "weird" groups that are finite and simple and usually have high order. There are \(26\) sporadic groups in total and they are the following (let me just throw names at you):
- Mathieu groups: \(M_{11}\), \(M_{12}\), \(M_{22}\), \(M_{23}\), \(M_{24}\).
- Janko groups: \(J_1\), \(J_2\), \(J_3\), \(J_4\).
- Conway groups: \(Co_1\), \(Co_2\), \(Co_3\).
- Suzuki group: \(Sz\).
- Baby Monster group: \(B\).
- Monster group: \(M\).
