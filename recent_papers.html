Some recent papers that caught my attention

The first paper that I found really cool Basically, Galois theory attaches to every polynomial with rational coefficients a finite group, its Galois group, which records the symmetries among the polynomial's roots. The inverse Galois problem turns this arround: given a finite group $G$, it asks if there exists a polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ whose Galois group is $G$? This problem has already been extensively studied, it is known that alternating groups, abelian groups and solvable groups are all realizable as Galois group of a finite Galois extension of $\mathbb{Q}$. Nonetheless, there are many, many, many more groups for which the problem remains open.

A natural place to test the problem is the finite simple groups. They are kind of the building blocks from which all finite groups are assembled. The classification of finite simple groups says that each finite simple group belongs to a few infinite families:
cyclic groups of prime order, alternating groups, Lie groups and sporadic groups.

That last familiy is the one we are interested in. To put it simply, sporadic groups are "weird" groups that are finite and simple and usually have high order.
